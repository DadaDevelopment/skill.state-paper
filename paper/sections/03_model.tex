\section{Formal model}
\label{sec:model}

This section fixes notation, states the two runtimes as algorithms, and
derives the accounting identity that the rest of the paper measures against.
The asymptotic argument is the original's\origin; our contribution here is to
show which of its terms the standard evaluation holds constant, and what the
identity becomes once that term is released.

\subsection{Notation}

An agent executes a task over discrete turns $t = 1, 2, \dots$ Let $P$ be the
task specification, $o_t$ the observation returned by the environment at turn
$t$, $a_t$ the action the agent takes, and $R_t$ its intermediate reasoning.
Write $\Sigma_t$ for the structured execution state and $C_t$ for the context
assembled for the model call at turn $t$. We use $|\cdot|$ for size in
tokens.

The \emph{horizon} $T$ is the number of turns the episode actually takes.
Whether $T$ is an input or an output is the distinction on which
\S\ref{sec:repro} turns, and we mark it explicitly: $T^{\text{env}}$ when the
environment fixes it, $T^{\text{ag}}$ when the agent stops of its own accord.

\subsection{Two context policies}

The transcript runtime appends every exchange, so its context at turn $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  C^{\text{tr}}_t \;=\; \bigl[\,P,\; (o_1, R_1, a_1), \dots,
  (o_{t-1}, R_{t-1}, a_{t-1}),\; o_t \,\bigr],
  \label{eq:ctx-transcript}
\end{equation}
which grows linearly in $t$, giving $|C^{\text{tr}}_t| = \mathcal{O}(t)$ and
cumulative prompt volume
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl|C^{\text{tr}}_t\bigr| \;=\; \mathcal{O}(T^2).
  \label{eq:sum-transcript}
\end{equation}

The structured-state runtime discards the history and carries only the state:
\begin{equation}
  C^{\text{st}}_t \;=\; \bigl[\,P,\; \Sigma_{t-1},\; o_t \,\bigr],
  \qquad
  \bigl|C^{\text{st}}_t\bigr| \;=\; \mathcal{O}\bigl(|P| + |\Sigma| + |o|\bigr),
  \label{eq:ctx-state}
\end{equation}
which is independent of $t$ whenever the state is bounded, so
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{t=1}^{T} \bigl|C^{\text{st}}_t\bigr| \;=\; \mathcal{O}(T).
  \label{eq:sum-state}
\end{equation}

Equations~\eqref{eq:sum-transcript} and~\eqref{eq:sum-state} are the
published argument, and we reproduce them without dispute\origin.

\subsection{The tail family that interpolates between them}

We generalise both into one family indexed by an integer $\tailk \ge 0$, the
number of most recent exchanges retained verbatim beside the state:
\begin{equation}
  C^{(\tailk)}_t \;=\; \bigl[\,P,\;
  (o_{t-\tailk}, R_{t-\tailk}, a_{t-\tailk}), \dots, (o_{t-1}, R_{t-1}, a_{t-1}),\;
  \Sigma_{t-1},\; o_t \,\bigr].
  \label{eq:ctx-tail}
\end{equation}
At $\tailk = 0$ this is exactly \eqref{eq:ctx-state}; as $\tailk \to t$ it
recovers \eqref{eq:ctx-transcript} with a redundant state block. The control
carries no state block at all and is therefore not a member of the family; we
report it separately throughout.

Every condition in this paper is a value of $\tailk$ in \eqref{eq:ctx-tail}.
This is what makes the comparison a position on a curve rather than a
contest between named systems.

\subsection{The runtimes as algorithms}

\begin{minipage}{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Transcript runtime (control)}
\label{alg:transcript}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require task specification $P$
\State $H \gets \varnothing$ \Comment{interaction history}
\For{$t = 1, 2, \dots$}
  \State receive observation $o_t$
  \State $C_t \gets [\,P,\; H,\; o_t\,]$
  \State generate $(R_t, a_t) \sim \pi(C_t)$
  \State execute $a_t$
  \State $H \gets H \,\Vert\, (o_t, R_t, a_t)$
  \If{agent declares completion} \State \textbf{break} \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}{0.48\textwidth}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{State runtime with tail $\tailk$}
\label{alg:state}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require spec $P$; schema $S$; tail $\tailk$
\State $\Sigma_0 \gets \varnothing$;\; $H \gets \varnothing$
\For{$t = 1, 2, \dots$}
  \State receive observation $o_t$
  \State $C_t \gets [\,P,\; \mathrm{tail}_{\tailk}(H),\; \Sigma_{t-1},\; o_t\,]$
  \State generate $(R_t, \Delta\Sigma_t, a_t) \sim \pi(C_t)$
  \If{$\mathrm{valid}_S(\Delta\Sigma_t)$}
    \State $\Sigma_t \gets \Sigma_{t-1} \oplus \Delta\Sigma_t$
  \Else
    \State $\Sigma_t \gets \Sigma_{t-1}$; return error to model
  \EndIf
  \State execute $a_t$;\; discard $R_t$
  \State $H \gets H \,\Vert\, (o_t, R_t, a_t)$
  \If{agent declares completion} \State \textbf{break} \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\end{minipage}

\medskip
\noindent
The two runtimes differ in exactly one respect: what line~4 assembles. The
merge $\oplus$ applies a schema-validated patch, and an invalid patch leaves
the state untouched. At $\tailk{=}0$ the tail is empty and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:state} is \SkillState{} as published. Both discard the
reasoning $R_t$; only Algorithm~\ref{alg:state} also drops the history from
the context.

The termination test in both (line~8 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:transcript} and
line~13 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:state}) is the agent's own declaration of
completion, not the expiry of an external counter. This is the only
structural difference from the original's formulation, where the loop runs
for a fixed $T^{\text{env}}$, and \S\ref{sec:repro} shows it is the
difference that matters.

\subsection{What the standard accounting omits}

Let $\mathcal{I}_t$ denote the information the agent requires at turn $t$ to
choose a correct action. This information can arrive by two routes: carried
in the context, or re-acquired from the environment by spending a turn on an
inspection action. Write $\rho$ for the fraction of turns spent on
re-acquisition.

A transcript agent has already paid for its history, because the history sits
in its prompt, so $\rho^{\text{tr}}$ is small. A state agent has discarded
whatever $\Sigma$ does not encode, so any needed fact outside $\Sigma$ must
be re-acquired, and $\rho^{(\tailk)}$ grows as $\tailk$ shrinks. Each
re-acquisition consumes a turn, so the realised horizon is inflated:
\begin{equation}
  T^{\text{ag}}(\tailk) \;\approx\; \frac{T_0}{1 - \rho(\tailk)},
  \label{eq:horizon-inflation}
\end{equation}
where $T_0$ is the turn count an agent with perfect recall would need.

Substituting \eqref{eq:horizon-inflation} into \eqref{eq:sum-state} gives the
quantity the original's asymptotics do not contain:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{t=1}^{T^{\text{ag}}} \bigl|C^{\text{st}}_t\bigr|
  \;=\; \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{T_0}{1-\rho}\right),
  \qquad \rho = \rho(\tailk).
  \label{eq:sum-state-agentic}
\end{equation}

The published comparison evaluates \eqref{eq:sum-state} against
\eqref{eq:sum-transcript} with $T = T^{\text{env}}$ held equal across arms.
That choice sets $\rho$ outside the model: with the turn count fixed by the
environment, re-acquisition cannot lengthen an episode, and
\eqref{eq:horizon-inflation} degenerates to $T^{\text{ag}} = T^{\text{env}}$
for every runtime, no matter how much was forgotten.

This is the paper's central formal point. The asymptotic advantage of
\eqref{eq:sum-state} lives in a variable ($T$) that the evaluation holds
constant, while the mechanism under test ($\rho$) acts on exactly that
variable. We measure $\rho$ directly in \S\ref{sec:mechanism} and
$T^{\text{ag}}$ in \S\ref{sec:capability}.

\subsection{Cost is not token count}

Let $\alpha$, $\gamma$, $\beta$ be the per-token prices of uncached input,
cached input and output. With $h_t$ the cached fraction of the prompt at
turn $t$, the billed cost of an episode is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Cost} \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{T}
  \Bigl[\, \alpha\,(1-h_t)\,|C_t| \;+\; \gamma\,h_t\,|C_t|
  \;+\; \beta\,|y_t| \,\Bigr],
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $y_t$ is the generated output, which for a state runtime includes the
patch $\Delta\Sigma_t$. On current providers $\gamma \ll \alpha < \beta$.

Two consequences follow, and both are measured in \S\ref{sec:economics}.
A transcript context is append-only, so $C^{\text{tr}}_t$ extends
$C^{\text{tr}}_{t-1}$ and $h_t \to 1$: the growing prompt of
\eqref{eq:sum-transcript} is billed mostly at the cheap rate $\gamma$. A
state block is rewritten each turn, so the prefix diverges at $\Sigma$ and
$h_t$ stays low despite the small $|C_t|$. Meanwhile maintaining state moves
tokens from the $|C_t|$ terms into $|y_t|$, which carries the highest price
$\beta$.

Hence the identity this work is built on: minimising $\sum_t |C_t|$ and
minimising \eqref{eq:cost} are different objectives, and on the corpus
measured here they have opposite solutions.
