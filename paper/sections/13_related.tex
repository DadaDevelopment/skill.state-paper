\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Structured state memory.} \SkillState{}~\citep{skillstate2026} is
the system under test and is described in \S\ref{sec:background}. The family it
belongs to (replacing history with a model-maintained structured
representation) shares the assumption we falsify: that the representation is a
sufficient statistic for the remaining work.

\paragraph{Deterministic execution state.} \Ledger{}~\citep{ledger2026}
maintains an objective record derived from completed interactions and adds it
to an otherwise unmodified agent that keeps its history. Our composition study
(\S\ref{sec:composition}) varies the boundary between the two designs. The
difference in setting matters and we state it plainly: \Ledger{} \emph{adds} a
deterministic layer to an agent with full history, whereas we ask what a
deterministic layer must contain when the history has been \emph{removed}.

\paragraph{Context management by compression.} Summarisation runtimes, sliding
windows and learned prompt compression~\citep{llmlingua2023} attack the same
growth problem by shrinking the transcript rather than replacing it. The
original's budget-matched controls report that at equal prompt size these score
well below structured state\origin, which is evidence that structure rather
than brevity carries its gains, a conclusion our results do not contradict.
Our finding is about what is lost when the transcript is discarded entirely,
not about how well it can be compressed.

\paragraph{Agentic reasoning loops.} The transcript baseline is
ReAct-style~\citep{react2022}: interleaved reasoning and acting with an
append-only history. Its cost profile under prefix caching is what makes the
comparison in \S\ref{sec:economics} non-obvious, since the growing prompt is
largely cache-served.

\paragraph{Benchmarks with programmatic verifiers.} We use Terminal-Bench,
whose tasks carry their own verifiers, for the reason given in
\S\ref{sec:setup}: a verdict must not depend on a model's judgement of its own
output. InterCode CTF~\citep{intercode2023} and
$\tau$-bench~\citep{taubench2024} are used by the original and share this
property.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology.} Our methodological contribution
(\S\ref{sec:pitfalls}) applies standard reproducibility practice (external
verdicts, pre-registered power, written retractions) to a genre where, in our
experience, the dominant error is not statistical but definitional: a column
that measures a property of the harness while being labelled a property of the
method.
