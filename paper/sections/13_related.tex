\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Structured state memory.} \SkillState{}~\citep{skillstate2026} is
the system under test and is described in \S\ref{sec:background}. The family it
belongs to (replacing history with a model-maintained structured
representation) shares the assumption we falsify: that the representation is a
sufficient statistic for the remaining work.

\paragraph{Deterministic execution state.} \Ledger{}~\citep{ledger2026}
maintains an objective record derived from completed interactions and adds it
to an otherwise unmodified agent that keeps its history. Our composition study
(\S\ref{sec:composition}) varies the boundary between the two designs. The
setting differs in a way worth stating outright: \Ledger{} \emph{adds} a
deterministic layer to an agent with full history, whereas we ask what a
deterministic layer must contain when the history has been \emph{removed}.

\paragraph{Context management by compression.} Summarisation runtimes, sliding
windows and learned prompt compression~\citep{llmlingua2023} attack the same
growth problem by shrinking the transcript instead of replacing it. The
original's budget-matched controls report that at equal prompt size these score
well below structured state\origin, which is evidence that structure rather
than brevity carries its gains, a conclusion our results do not contradict.
Our finding is about what is lost when the transcript is discarded entirely,
not about how well it can be compressed.

\paragraph{Harness components in isolation.} \citet{harnessdesign2026} run 176
matched settings that vary planning, action space and context management
independently inside one fixed execution loop, across four models on
SWE-Bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1. Their elision-and-recall context
condition is the closest published analogue to our tail axis
(\S\ref{sec:capability}): both cut the transcript and give the agent a
mechanism to pull back what was cut. Their recall channel is reported to add
little once elision is in place, which mirrors a finding of our own: the
plugin's \texttt{verified\_*} coverage counters read identically zero across
every run in the composition series (\S\ref{sec:composition}), a channel built
to be checked that nothing in \VRuns{} runs exercises. We read this as the
same failure mode arrived at from two different designs, not a coincidence of
naming.

\paragraph{Context blindness.} \citet{vista2026} argue that a model cannot
reliably infer block size, recency, or remaining budget from the prompt alone,
and introduce a visible internal-state channel so the agent can see what it
is managing. Our mechanism result (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}) is consistent with
this diagnosis without needing to invoke it: a failing state-only run spends a
majority of its commands re-deriving facts it already produced, which is what
context blindness predicts an agent under-informed about its own history
would do. Their fix operates on the model's view of its context; ours
(\S\ref{sec:composition}) operates on who writes the record in the first
place. The two are not competing, and \S\ref{sec:conclusion} names their
combination as a direction we have not measured.

\paragraph{Lossless recursive context.} \citet{lcm2026} maintain a
deterministic tree of hierarchical summaries with lossless pointers back to
the original text, letting an agent recurse into a summary node without
discarding the ability to retrieve what was summarised. This is the same
underlying claim as \Ledger{}'s in our setting (a deterministic layer can
carry information the model's own free-text summary would drop) applied
recursively rather than as one additional layer. It suggests the boundary we
study in \S\ref{sec:composition}, model-written versus runtime-written state,
is not the only axis available; a deterministic layer can also vary in depth.

\paragraph{Agentic reasoning loops.} The transcript baseline is
ReAct-style~\citep{react2022}: interleaved reasoning and acting with an
append-only history. Its cost profile under prefix caching is what makes the
comparison in \S\ref{sec:economics} non-obvious, since the growing prompt is
largely cache-served.

\paragraph{Benchmarks with programmatic verifiers.} We use Terminal-Bench,
whose tasks carry their own verifiers, for the reason given in
\S\ref{sec:setup}: a verdict must not depend on a model's judgement of its own
output. InterCode CTF~\citep{intercode2023} and
$\tau$-bench~\citep{taubench2024} are used by the original and share this
property.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology.} Our methodological contribution
(\S\ref{sec:pitfalls}) applies standard reproducibility practice (external
verdicts, pre-registered power, written retractions) to a genre where, in our
experience, the dominant error is definitional, not statistical: a column
labelled as a property of the method actually measures the harness instead.
