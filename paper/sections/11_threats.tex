\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{One model family.} Every run uses \texttt{\EOneModel}. A paired
pilot on a stronger model from the same provider solved no task the small
model could not, at \num{22.7}$\times$ the median cost per cell\live, which
is why the series was not moved to it. That pilot covers 7--8 tasks and
settles a budget question, not a transfer question. We claim nothing about
models from other families, and in particular nothing about whether a model
with stronger instruction-following would maintain state well enough to avoid
the failure mode of \S\ref{sec:mechanism}.

\paragraph{One corpus, and its horizon range.} Under the control,
Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks use a median of roughly \SI{5}{\percent} of the
context window\live. The window is therefore rarely the binding constraint,
which is the regime in which a memory mechanism should be least necessary. A
pilot on the longer Terminal-Bench 4.0 shows a median peak of
\SI{66}{\percent} of the window\live, and there the same mechanism appears an
order of magnitude stronger (median repetition \num{3.26} against the
control's \num{1.02}). That pilot is one trial on six tasks and is not a
result. The honest statement of scope is that our finding holds where the
window is not saturated; the direction of the effect in the saturated regime
is measured but not established.

\paragraph{Implementation fidelity.} Our state block is thicker per step than
the original reports\live, so the absolute cost magnitudes are upper bounds
(\S\ref{sec:repro}). Both corrections move against our headline result.

\paragraph{Tariff transfer in the reproduction.} Section~\ref{sec:repro}
applies our measured prices to the original's published token counts. The
order of magnitude does not depend on the exact multipliers, which are an
estimate and are labelled as one.

\paragraph{Contamination not detectable in principle.} The quarantine rule
(\S\ref{sec:pitfalls}) keys on errored turns. A provider degradation below
the error-visibility floor would not be caught. The bound on the damage is
that the cost results do not depend on verdicts at all, and that the
capability result survived removal of every cell the rule did catch.

\paragraph{Two unrunnable tasks.} Two of the 89 tasks fail during container
setup in every condition\live{} and are absent from all arms equally. This
removes two tasks from the corpus; it does not favour any condition.

\paragraph{The composition study carries one trial.}
Section~\ref{sec:composition} reports \VRuns{} runs at a single trial,
against three for E1. Given this project's own history (an effect significant
at two trials that died at the third, \S\ref{sec:pitfalls}), the individual
rung-to-rung steps there are provisional. The end-to-end comparison and the
direction of the cost trend are consistent across every rung, which is why
they are stated at all, but no claim in this paper's abstract or conclusion
rests on them.

\paragraph{No tail-only arm.} The four E1 conditions are a control with no
state block and three state-bearing arms at different tails. There is no arm
carrying the last $\tailk$ exchanges and \emph{no} state block. Without that
arm, \S\ref{sec:capability} can show that restoring history removes the
deficit, but it cannot separate how much of the remaining behaviour at
$\tailk{=}6$ comes from the state block and how much from the history beside
it. Table~\ref{tab:totals} contains a hint that this distinction matters:
\StateK{6} completes the series in fewer total turns than the control, which
a purely additive account of the two components does not obviously predict.
We flag this as unexplained instead of interpreting it, and the arm belongs
in the next series.
